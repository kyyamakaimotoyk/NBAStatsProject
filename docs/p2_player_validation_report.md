# P2 — Player Counting-Stat Projection: Validation & Calibration Report

P2 = the P1 protocol extended with (a) playoff/play-in/Cup-final games — as training rows, an IS_PLAYOFF feature, and a separate playoff error breakout — and (b) a fourth season, adding the train-2022-23 → test-2023-24 fold (2021-22 becomes the rolling warm-up).

Generated by `experiments/p1_player_validation.py`. Raw numbers: `outputs/p1_player_validation.json`; correlation matrices: `outputs/p1_feature_target_correlations.csv` (feature x target) and `outputs/p1_feature_feature_correlations.csv`.

**Task**: predict per-game MIN + 10 counting stats for the top-8 players per team (by rolling minutes).

## Models & method, in plain terms

**`naive`** — the player's average over his last 10 games, per stat. The floor any method must clear.

**`marcel`** — the must-beat baseline, adapted from Tom Tango's "Marcel the Monkey" forecasting system (baseball's canonical dumb-but-strong projection: *a player is what he's recently been, regressed toward the mean* — deliberately the smartest forecast you can make without machine learning). Ours works per-36-minutes so that playing-time changes don't contaminate the skill estimate:

1. **Projected minutes** = 0.5·(L5 avg) + 0.3·(L10 avg) + 0.2·(season avg).
2. **Per-36 rate** for each stat = 0.45·(L10 per-36) + 0.35·(season per-36) + 0.2·(previous-season per-36), NaN weights renormalized.
3. **Shrinkage**: blended rate is pulled toward the league-average per-36 rate with weight α = GP/(GP+10) — a 5-game sample keeps only α=0.33 of its own signal, a 40-game sample keeps 0.80. This is the "regression to the mean" step that makes Marcel hard to beat: small samples mostly become the league average.
4. **Projection** = projected minutes × shrunk rate / 36; 3PM capped at 3PA, FTM at FTA.

If XGBoost cannot beat this with paired significance, the ML layer is not earning its complexity — that is the pass/fail criterion of this report.

**`xgb`** — the approved production candidate, a *two-stage* model because a counting stat is mechanically (minutes played) × (production per minute), and the two parts have different drivers: minutes are a coach's rotation decision (role, blowout risk, absences), rates are skill × matchup. Stage 1 predicts minutes. Stage 2 predicts production: squared-error models on per-36 rates for PTS/REB/AST (minutes-weighted), and Poisson models for STL/BLK/TOV/FG3A/FTA (see below). 3PM/FTM are not modeled directly: predicted attempts × the player's shrunken season make-rate, which guarantees makes ≤ attempts.

### Why Poisson for the low-count stats

Steals, blocks, turnovers and attempt counts are small non-negative integers (0, 1, 2 …). A squared-error regressor treats "predicted 0.7 steals" as a point on a continuous line — it can go negative and says nothing about *how often* the player gets 0, 1, or 2. The Poisson distribution is the natural model for "number of events in a fixed exposure at rate λ":

    P(X = k) = λᵏ · e^(−λ) / k!

where λ is the expected count. We model λ = (per-36 rate from features) × (minutes/36) by giving XGBoost the `count:poisson` objective with log(minutes/36) as an *exposure offset* (`base_margin`) — training uses actual minutes as exposure, prediction plugs in the stage-1 predicted minutes. The payoff: λ is not just a point estimate, it implies a full probability distribution over 0/1/2/… — which the Poisson-calibration section then tests against reality, and which later feeds fantasy questions like "P(2+ blocks tonight)".

## Poisson frequency calibration (XGB, final fold)

For the Poisson-modeled stats, λ implies a full distribution over 0/1/2/… — this section tests whether those implied frequencies match reality. Rows bucket player-games by predicted λ. **emp_p0 / emp_p1 / emp_p2plus** = the observed share of games in the bucket where the player recorded exactly 0, exactly 1, or 2+ of the stat. **imp_p0 / imp_p1 / imp_p2plus** = the shares the model implies, i.e. Poisson probabilities averaged over the bucket: imp_p0 = mean of e^(−λ), imp_p1 = mean of λ·e^(−λ), imp_p2plus = 1 − imp_p0 − imp_p1. A calibrated model has emp ≈ imp in every cell. **Bins with n<30 are suppressed** (too small to estimate frequencies) and listed under each table — that is why a stat may show no [3.0,inf) row: nothing is capped, the model just (correctly) almost never predicts λ that high — no NBA player projects to 3+ steals per game (league leaders average ~2).
